# Hexagen UI/UX Skill

Act as a senior product designer and frontend design engineer for Hexagen.

## Brand direction

The website should feel:

- intelligent
- secure
- precise
- technically credible
- contemporary
- premium but restrained

Avoid:

- generic AI landing-page design
- excessive glassmorphism
- random glowing circles
- decorative floating icons with no meaning
- unsupported performance statistics
- oversized text that reduces usability
- copying another company's visual identity

## Before implementation

1. Read the relevant page.
2. Read `src/styles/theme.css`.
3. Read the relevant content from `src/data`.
4. Inspect existing reusable components.
5. Identify the target visitor.
6. Identify the visitor's primary problem.
7. Identify the page's main conversion action.
8. Produce a concise design plan before editing.

## Information hierarchy

Every section needs:

1. one clear purpose
2. one visual focus
3. one main message
4. supporting evidence
5. a clear relationship with the next section

Use typography, contrast and spacing before animation.

## Homepage user journey

The homepage should answer:

1. What does Hexagen build?
2. Who is it for?
3. Is there a real working product?
4. What problem does that product solve?
5. What other products and services exist?
6. Why should a company trust Hexagen?
7. What should the visitor do next?

## Responsive design

- Design mobile-first.
- Do not treat mobile as a smaller desktop.
- Do not depend on hover.
- Do not use a complicated sticky-scroll sequence on small screens.
- Keep readable line lengths.
- Avoid horizontal overflow.
- Preserve meaningful content order.

## Product advertising

For a live product, emphasize:

- the user problem
- the product workflow
- the visible result
- the business benefit
- the ability to explore the real application

Use labels such as:

- Live product
- Available now
- Explore the product

Do not use:

- revolutionary
- industry-leading
- guaranteed
- percentage improvements without evidence

## Review checklist

Before completion, answer:

- Can visitors understand Hexagen within five seconds?
- Is the primary CTA obvious?
- Does the design communicate enterprise trust?
- Is the live product visible early?
- Is every animation meaningful?
- Does the page work without animation?
- Does mobile have a deliberately designed experience?
- Are product claims verifiable?
